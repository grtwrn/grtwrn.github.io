The Louis: MIDI Trumpet

An affordable MIDI trumpet built to look, feel and sound like the real thing, so remote-learning students could practice without the cost or noise of an actual trumpet. Real valve springs and mouthpiece, a 3D-printed body, an I2S mic reading the mouthpiece buzz, valve sensors, and a Raspberry Pi + Teensy for signal processing, all for under $150 in parts.

Senior capstone at Brown (ENGN 1001), April 2021, with Riad Hallal, Nathan Plano and Albin Wells.

Yale Shuttle Tracker

A live map, stop-by-stop arrival times and a trip planner for Yale's Downtowner shuttles. It polls the shuttle GPS feed every 5 seconds and builds its own history of arrivals, dwell times and travel times between stops. Predictions are calibrated from what the buses actually did on that stretch of route at that hour and day of the week, not from the published timetable. The planner routes between any two points, including the walking legs.

A single Node service with a React front end and SQLite, plus automated map and data checks that catch bad predictions before riders see them.

yale-shuttle.fly.dev

Status: live, 2026

Future You: Attune

A sensing plugin for MIT Media Lab's Future You, where people talk with an AI simulation of their older self. Future You builds that persona only from what you type; Attune also watches how you answer: face (MediaPipe blendshapes), voice (pitch, loudness, pauses) and heart rate from a Bluetooth strap. The output is an affect profile and a persona prompt, so the future-self conversation knows which topics made you tense, energised, hesitant or calm.

Signals, not verdicts: every feature is measured against your own 45-second resting baseline, it never outputs emotion labels, and your self-report overrides the sensors when they disagree. All sensing runs locally in the browser.

Status: in development
